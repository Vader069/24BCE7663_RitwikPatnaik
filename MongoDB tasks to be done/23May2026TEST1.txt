Questions:
1.
[JOINS] List the name of each employee along with their job title and department name.
2.
[SUBQUERY] Find all employees whose salary is higher than the average salary of the 'IT' department.
3.
[GROUP BY] Calculate the total salary expenditure for each department name (not ID).
4.
[HAVING] Display departments that have more than 2 employees and an average salary above 70,000.
5.
[SELF JOIN] Retrieve a list of employee names and their respective manager's names.
6.
[ORDER BY] Find the top 3 highest-paid employees and list them in descending order.
7.
[JOINS + WHERE] Find all Developers working in the 'San Francisco' location.
8.
[GROUP BY] Find the number of employees hired each year.
9.
[HAVING] List job titles where the maximum salary of an employee in that role is less than 100,000.
10.
[SUBQUERY] Find the names of employees who work in the same department as 'David'.
11.
[MULTI-TABLE JOIN] Show all department names even if they have no employees assigned to them.
12.
[COMPLEX FILTER] Find employees who were hired between 2018 and 2020 and earn more than 60,000.
13.
[CORRELATED SUBQUERY] Find employees who earn more than the average salary of their own department.
14.
[JOINS] Find the job title and salary of the employee with ID 504.
15.
[GROUP BY] For each manager (by ID), find the minimum salary of the people reporting to them.
16.
[SUBQUERY] List departments that do not have any 'Analysts'.
17.
[AGGREGATION] Find the difference between the highest and lowest salary in the company.
18.
[JOIN + ORDER BY] Display employee names and hire dates, sorted by department name (A-Z) and then by salary (High to Low).
19.
[HAVING] Identify managers (by ID) who manage more than 2 people.
20.
[SUBQUERY] Find the employee(s) with the third-highest salary.
21.
[LEFT JOIN] List all job titles and the number of employees currently holding that job.
22.
[JOIN + LIKE] Find employees in the 'Finance' department whose names contain the letter 'i'.
23.
[MATH + GROUP BY] Calculate the 10% bonus amount for each employee and show the total bonus per department.
24.
[SUBQUERY] List the name of the department that pays the highest total salary.
25.
[JOINS] Find all employees who report to 'Charlie'.

Answers :
1. select name,job_title from employees,jobs where employees.job_id=jobs.job_id;
2. select name from employees where salary > (Select avg(salary) from employees where dept_id = 20);
3. select sum(salary),dept_name from employees,departments where employees.dept_id=departments.dept_id group by dept_name ;
4. select dept_name from departments,employees where departments.dept_id=employees.dept_id group by departments.dept_id having count(emp_id)>2 and avg(salary)>70000; 
5. SELECT e.name , m.name FROM Employees e LEFT JOIN Employees m ON e.manager_id = m.emp_id;
6. select salary from employees order by salary desc limit 3;
7. select * from employees e,departments d where e.dept_id=d.dept_id and d.location="San Francisco";
8. select count(emp_id),extract(year from hire_date) from employees group by extract(year from hire_date);
9. select job_title, max(salary) from jobs,employees where jobs.job_id=employees.job_id group by job_title having max(salary)<100000;
10. select name from employees where dept_id=(select dept_id from employees where name='David');
11. select e.name, d.dept_name from employees e left join departments d on d.dept_id = e.dept_id;
12. SELECT name, salary, hire_date FROM Employees WHERE hire_date BETWEEN '2018-01-01' AND '2020-12-31' AND salary > 60000;
13. SELECT e1.name, e1.salary, e1.dept_id FROM Employees e1 WHERE e1.salary > (SELECT AVG(e2.salary) FROM Employees e2 WHERE e1.dept_id = e2.dept_id);
14. select j.job_title,e.name from jobs j,employees e where j.job_id=e.job_id and e.emp_id=504;
15. SELECT manager_id, MIN(salary) AS min_salary FROM Employees WHERE manager_id IS NOT NULL GROUP BY manager_id;
16. SELECT d.dept_name FROM Departments d WHERE d.dept_id NOT IN (SELECT e.dept_id FROM Employees e JOIN Jobs j ON e.job_id = j.job_id WHERE j.job_title = 'Analyst');
17. select max(salary) - min(salary) AS SAL_DIFF from Employees;
18. select e.name, e.hire_date from employees e join departments d on e.dept_id = d.dept_id order by d.dept_name asc, e.salary desc;
19. select manager_id from employees where manager_id is not null group by manager_id having count(emp_id) > 2;
